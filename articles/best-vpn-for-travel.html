Best VPN for Travel: Hotel & Airport Wi-Fi Safety

By the Privacy Plain English Team · September 21, 2026 · 10 min read · Updated September 2026

You land at 11pm, the hotel Wi-Fi asks for your room number, and fifty strangers share the same network password. This is the single best reason an ordinary person buys a VPN — and the scenario most "best VPN" lists barely test for. So we ranked providers on what actually matters on the road.

What Travelers Actually Need From a VPN

Travel use is different from home use. At home, you set it once and forget it. On the road, you need:

  • Dead-simple mobile apps. You'll be connecting from a phone in an airport, half-awake. One-tap connect, obvious server picker, no hunting through menus.
  • Reliable connections on captive-portal Wi-Fi. Hotel and airport networks make you click through a login page before the internet works. Some VPN apps fight these portals; good ones handle them gracefully (and let you connect after the portal login).
  • Generous device limits. Phone + laptop + tablet + travel companion's phone adds up fast. 5 simultaneous connections is the floor; 10 (or unlimited) is comfortable.
  • Fast nearby servers everywhere. You'll want a server near wherever you land — good coverage across North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific matters more than a big total server count.
  • Auto-connect on untrusted Wi-Fi. The feature that makes travel privacy automatic instead of a chore you forget.
  • The trust basics, always. Audited no-logs policy, kill switch, WireGuard support, leak protection — the six criteria from our beginner explainer. Non-negotiable.

How We Evaluated (Our Methodology)

Honesty first: we did not run a lab with stopwatches. Here's exactly what we did, so you can judge the picks:

  1. Documentation review. We read each provider's published no-logs audit reports (checking the auditing firm's name and the report date — within the last two years), jurisdiction, protocol support, kill-switch availability on mobile and desktop, and stated device limits.
  2. Feature check against travel criteria. We verified auto-connect on untrusted networks, captive-portal handling guidance, and split tunneling in each provider's support documentation and current apps.
  3. Real-world usage notes. We aggregated widely reported user experiences with hotel Wi-Fi and streaming reliability from reputable tech publications' 2025–2026 reviews, and weighted providers with consistent reports over ones with mixed reports.
  4. Pricing check. We compared actual 1–2 year plan prices (dollars per month), not headline discount percentages.

We re-verify these picks quarterly. VPN apps, ownership, and audit statuses change — a recommendation from 2024 is not a recommendation today. Anything here that has changed since publication gets corrected; stale travel picks erode trust fast.

Our Picks

Best overall for travelers: NordVPN

NordVPN earns the top spot because it checks every travel box with the least friction: genuinely simple mobile apps with one-tap connect, auto-connect on untrusted Wi-Fi, 10 simultaneous devices, WireGuard-based NordLynx protocol for fast connections, and an audited no-logs policy (multiple independent audits, most recently within the last two years). Its Meshnet and threat-protection extras are nice but optional — the core travel experience is what wins: connect in seconds from a hotel lobby without thinking.

Typical pricing lands in the $3–5/month range on 2-year plans. Watch for the upsell maze at checkout (add-on bundles you don't need) — the base plan is the right one for most travelers.

Best for ease of use: ExpressVPN

If you want the absolute simplest app — big power button, server list, nothing else to learn — ExpressVPN is the pick. Its Lightway protocol connects noticeably fast (often under two seconds), which matters when you're hopping between airport and hotel networks. Audited no-logs policy, RAM-only servers, kill switch on all platforms, and straightforward captive-portal behavior.

The trade-off is price: ExpressVPN typically costs more than competitors (roughly $5–8/month on longer plans) and allows fewer simultaneous devices (8). You're paying for polish. For a nervous first-time VPN user, that polish is worth it.

Best budget option: Surfshark

Surfshark is the value standout: unlimited simultaneous devices (genuinely unlimited — cover your whole travel party on one account), WireGuard support, auto-connect on untrusted networks, audited no-logs policy, and 2-year plans that routinely price around $2–3/month. The apps are slightly less refined than the two picks above, but perfectly usable.

One caveat: Surfshark's ownership sits under a larger group, and its server network, while large, has more variable speeds in our aggregated reports — stick to nearby servers and you'll be fine.

Worth mentioning: Proton VPN (best free tier for light travel)

If you travel rarely — one trip a year — Proton VPN's free tier is the only free option we'd recommend: no data cap shenanigans from a shady operator, just a limited free plan (fewer servers, medium speeds) from a company with a strong audit record and transparent funding via paid plans. It won't unblock streaming reliably, but for safe hotel Wi-Fi browsing it does the job. Paid plans add the full server network at competitive prices.

Why these four and not others? Every pick above has a published independent no-logs audit, a kill switch on mobile and desktop, WireGuard or equivalent modern protocol, and at least 5 simultaneous devices. Providers that failed any of those — or that we couldn't verify — didn't make the list, regardless of commission rates.

How to Use a VPN on Hotel Wi-Fi (Captive Portal Tips)

Hotel Wi-Fi's login page ("enter your room number and last name") is the #1 travel VPN headache. The portal blocks all traffic — including your VPN tunnel — until you authenticate. The fix, every time:

  1. Disconnect the VPN first (or leave it off until you're online).
  2. Join the hotel Wi-Fi network.
  3. Open your browser — the portal page should appear. If it doesn't, try visiting any plain-HTTP site (search "portal login test page") to trigger it.
  4. Complete the login, wait for confirmation that you're online.
  5. Now connect the VPN. Verify with a quick IP check if you're cautious.

Before your trip, enable auto-connect on untrusted networks in your VPN app's settings — on many apps this handles steps gracefully after the first portal login of the day. Also enable any "allow local network / captive portal" exception if your app offers one.

Travel Edge Cases: Airline, Streaming & Banking Blocks Abroad

  • Airline Wi-Fi portals work like hotel portals — authenticate first, VPN second. Note that in-flight Wi-Fi is slow; a VPN adds a little overhead, so disconnect if you just need basic browsing.
  • Streaming abroad is hit-or-miss. Connect to a server in your home country, but expect some services to detect the VPN IP and block it. Switching to a different server in the same country often works — providers rotate IPs constantly in this cat-and-mouse game.
  • Banking apps may flag a foreign VPN IP as suspicious and lock you out. Before a trip, add your bank's app to split tunneling (route it outside the VPN) or be ready to disconnect briefly. Better: set up your bank's app and 2FA before you leave.
  • Countries that restrict VPNs. A small number of countries restrict or ban VPN use. Check local law before you travel — and note that in such places, even downloading the app on arrival may be difficult, so install before you go.

What to Pack: The 5-Minute Pre-Trip Privacy Checklist

  1. Install your VPN app on every device you're bringing — and log in at home, not on airport Wi-Fi.
  2. Turn on the kill switch on each device.
  3. Enable auto-connect on untrusted Wi-Fi.
  4. Run one leak test at home so you know what "working" looks like.
  5. Turn on two-factor authentication for your email and bank before you leave.
  6. Back up your phone (cloud or computer) — a lost device abroad is worse than any Wi-Fi snooper.

FAQ

Is hotel Wi-Fi really dangerous, or is that marketing?
It's a real but specific risk. On shared hotel Wi-Fi, unencrypted traffic can be intercepted by others on the network, and rogue hotspots ("Hotel_WiFi_FREE") can impersonate the real thing. HTTPS protects most of your logins, but a VPN closes the remaining gaps — DNS queries, metadata, and non-HTTPS traffic. It's a reasonable precaution, not paranoia.
Do I need the VPN on for my whole trip?
Keep it on for any Wi-Fi you don't control — hotels, airports, cafés, conference centers. On your phone's cellular data, the risk is much lower (cellular encryption is decent), so feel free to disconnect to save battery. The auto-connect feature handles this judgment for you.
Will a VPN drain my phone battery while traveling?
Slightly — encryption costs some power, roughly a few percent over a full day of use. Carry a power bank (you should anyway) and don't worry about it.
Can I just use the free hotel VPN or the airport's "secure" Wi-Fi?
No. "Secure" hotel Wi-Fi usually just means password-protected, and everyone has the same password. Your own VPN from a provider you chose is the only tunnel you should trust.
